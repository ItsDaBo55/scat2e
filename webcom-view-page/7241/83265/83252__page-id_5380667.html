Glossary

access point the area where an IV needle, catheter, or lock is placed for access to a patient’s vein

 

accidental fluid overload unintentional administration of excess IV fluid, which may result in electrolyte imbalance or hypervolemia

 

accreditation the process by which an official organization gives authority when recognized standards have been met or exceeded or sanctions when not met

 

Accreditation Council for Pharmacy Education (ACPE) an organization that works in collaboration with the American Society of Health-System Pharmacists (ASHP) and other pharmacy organizations to set accreditation standards for pharmacy technician education and training programs as well as colleges of pharmacy; provider of continuing pharmacy education for pharmacists and pharmacy technicians

 

acidity one of the chemical properties of a parenteral solution; any solution with a pH less than 7.0 is considered to be acidic

 

acquired immune deficiency syndrome (AIDS) a transmittable, retroviral disease due to infection by the human immunodeficiency virus (HIV); HIV attacks certain types of lymphocytes, severely impairing cell-mediated immunity in patients who have the disease

 

activating a syringe an action taken by sterile compounding personnel to release the slight negative pressure inside a manufactured syringe prior to its use; this pressure occurs at the site where the rubber-tipped piston plunger meets the inner barrel and is relieved by rapidly pulling down and pushing up on the flat knob of the plunger

 

active pharmaceutical ingredient (API) a substance in a medication that exerts pharmacological activity

 

acute flaccid myelitis (AFM) a rare neurological condition that causes muscle weakness and paralysis

 

Add-A-Vial a brand of IVPB solution in which a specially designed bag and vial either screw or snap together; IVPB is activated by a nurse just prior to patient administration

 

ADD-Vantage a brand of IVPB solution in which a specially designed bag and vial either screw or snap together; IVPB is activated by a nurse just prior to patient administration

 

addEASE a brand of IVPB solution in which a specially designed bag and vial either screw or snap together; IVPB is activated by a nurse just prior to patient administration

 

addiction a physical and psychological dependence on a chemical substance such as a narcotic or a narcotic analgesic

 

additive a medication, electrolyte, or other ingredient that is injected into a parenteral solution to prepare a CSP

 

addressograph a machine in the institutional healthcare setting that stamps the patient’s name, identification number, hospital room number, date of birth, and other information onto a medication order

 

adjuvant chemotherapy a type of chemotherapy administered after surgical removal or reduction of the primary tumor or tumors

 

administration time the length of time (usually prescribed in milliliters per hour) over which a parenteral medication is to be administered to a patient; see also infusion rate

 

admission order the first medication order written by a physician; often occurs while a patient is in the emergency department or just after a patient has been admitted to a hospital room

 

adulterated weakened or lessened in purity by the addition of improper or substandard ingredients

 

adulterated drug a drug that has been mishandled or improperly packaged; can lead to contamination, impurity, poor quality, or incorrect potency

 

adult-strength formulation the most common strength of a drug as supplied by the drug’s manufacturer; the strength of a drug that must be diluted (i.e., reduced in concentration or strength) prior to its use in the pediatric patient population

 

adverse drug event (ADE) an unintended or unexpected negative consequence of a drug, which may or may not result in harmful consequences to a patient; also known as an adverse drug reaction (ADR) or an adverse event (AE); may or may not be related to a medication error

 

adverse drug reaction (ADR) an unintended or un­expected negative consequence of a drug, which may or may not result in harmful consequences to a patient; also known as an adverse drug event (ADE) or an adverse event (AE)

 

adverse reaction an unexpected, undesirable effect caused by a drug

 

aerosol a small liquid or solid particles that is temporarily suspended in air or in another gaseous medium

 

aerosolization the undesired release of small particles or spray into the air from a vial or a wCSP; a fine mist released from a chemotherapy vial due to incorrect use of air pressure during compounding, which results in dangerous contamination of a BSC

 

Agriculture Improvement Act a 2018 act that removed hemp, defined as the plant of cannabis sativa L. and any component derived from this plant, with a THC concentration of no more than 0.3 percent on a dry weight basis as a controlled substance under the CSA; commonly referred to as the 2018 Farm Bill

 

air changes per hour (ACPH) the number of times per hour the air in a cleanroom suite or C–SCA is exchanged through an HVAC system

 

air intake grills the grills that run along the interior sides of a BSC’s work surface; these grills pull contaminated air from within the work space away from the compounder and into the exhaust HEPA filter

 

air quality the level of airborne contaminants in a sterile compounding environment

 

air sampling the collection and incubation of air samples in a growth media to detect microbial growth

 

airborne contaminant any microorganism (such as a bacterium or a virus) or particle (such as dust) that may be airborne, potentially transmitting illness or disease or causing contamination to a PEC or a CSP

 

airflow testing a quality assurance test conducted by certification personnel every six months to ensure adequate ACPH in ISO-classified areas and unclassified C–SCAs

 

alchemist a skilled compounding artisan

 

alchemy an early science aimed at transmuting base metals into gold; a term loosely used to describe the blend of supernatural beliefs and medieval medicine that comprised the art and science of early pharmacy practices

 

alcohol-based hand rub an agent that is shown to reduce the microbial bioburden on skin

 

alkalinity one of the chemical properties of a parenteral solution; any solution with a pH greater than 7.0 is considered to be alkaline

 

alkaloid a naturally occurring, nitrogen-based compound

 

alkylating agent a cytotoxic drug that acts by binding to nucleic acids and inhibiting cell division

 

allergic reaction an undesirable immune system response to a substance such as an antibiotic; response can range from mild symptoms such as hives and itching to severe, life-threatening symptoms causing respiratory distress; see anaphylaxis

 

alligation method a problem-solving method that may be used to find the amount of each ingredient needed to make a mixture of a given percentage of drug; a method of solving math problems related to the mixture of two ingredients; also called, simply, alligation

 

aluminum needle sheath a small, aluminum ring that encircles a vented needle; during sterile compounding procedures, the sheath is firmly seated in a vial stopper, thereby allowing air to vent around the needle and out of the vial

 

American Pharmaceutical Association (APhA) an organization whose focus is on the quality education, practice tools, resources, and advocacy of pharmacy professionals; the first-established professional society of pharmacists in the United States; organization now called the American Pharmacists Association

 

American Society of Health-System Pharmacists (ASHP) a pharmacy professional organization that provides advocacy, continuing education, and drug information for pharmacy professionals and that collaborates with ACPE to accredit pharmacy technician education and training programs

 

ammonia compound an ammonia-based solution that is used for surface disinfection

 

ampule a small container made of thin, medical-grade glass that contains certain sterile parenteral medications

 

ampule body the reservoir that holds sterile fluid within a glass ampule

 

ampule breaker a small plastic cap that fits over the head of an ampule and is used to snap the ampule neck, thus reducing the compounder’s risk of injury from glass shards

 

ampule head the uppermost part of a glass ampule that must be removed by sterile compounding personnel to access the fluid within the ampule body

 

ampule neck the tapered area of an ampule that separates the body and head of the ampule; the site where an ampule must be broken to remove the ampule head

 

ampule shoulder the uppermost part of an ampule body; the site just below the tapered neck of an ampule

 

amulet a small, decorative object, often worn around the neck, that is used to bring good luck or protection to the wearer

 

anaphylaxis a potentially life-threatening allergic reaction characterized by tongue or throat swelling and respiratory distress

 

anodyne a medication used to treat pain

 

anorexia the symptom of poor appetite; not to be confused with the eating disorder Anorexia nervosa

 

antagonist a substance that interferes with or inhibits the physiological action of another; for example, a hormone antagonist slows the growth of a hormone-dependent tumor

 

anteroom an ISO Class 8 (or cleaner) transition area where garbing, hand hygiene, CSP supply staging, and other preparatory activities take place prior to entering the buffer room

 

antibacterial drug a drug that is used to kill or inhibit the growth of bacteria

 

antibiotic drug a drug used to treat infections caused by bacteria

 

antibiotic of last resort an antibiotic used to treat patients who have bacterial infections that are resistant to all other known treatments

 

antibiotic-resistant bacteria bacteria that have evolved to the point of being resistant to known antibiotic therapy; a superbug such as methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA), vancomycin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (VRSA), carbapenem-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae (CRKP), carbapenem-resistant Enterobacteriaceae (CRE), and New Delhi metallo-beta-lactamase (NDM-1)

 

antidote a medication that counteracts a specific poison

 

antiemetic a drug that suppresses or relieves nausea and vomiting

 

antimetabolite medication a cytotoxic drug that disrupts metabolic pathways, thus preventing target cancer cells from synthesizing components essential for their growth and replication

 

antimicrobial agent a substance or chemical compound capable of killing or inhibiting the growth of various microorganisms

 

antimicrobial resistance (AMR) an evolutionary process in which an organism such as a bacterium develops resistance to treatment with an antimicrobial drug, thus rendering the drug or treatment ineffective

 

antimicrotubule agent a cytotoxic drug that disrupts the formation of cellular components required for mitosis, thus interfering with cell division and inhibiting growth; also called a mitotic inhibitor

 

antineoplastic hazardous drug a type of medication that destroys neoplasms or tumors

 

antineoplastic medication an agent that retards or prevents the growth of cancer cells; the terms cytotoxic and antineoplastic are often used interchangeably to describe chemotherapy drugs

 

antiseptic an agent capable of reducing or preventing infection

 

aplastic anemia a condition in which the bone marrow fails to produce new red blood cells, white blood cells, and platelets

 

apothecary a shop in which medicines were compounded by skilled artisans using herbs and other natural ingredients; the artisans themselves were often called apothecaries

 

apothecary system of measurement a virtually obsolete system of measurement used predominantly by pharmacies prior to the metric system; originated in Roman culture

 

appropriate medication dosage a dose of medication that is appropriate for the treatment of a patient’s medical condition and is based on the manufacturer’s dosing recommendation; a dose of medication that may be determined based on a patient’s body weight, age, gender, liver and kidney functions, and various other factors

 

aromatherapy a therapy or treatment that uses the fragrant oils extracted from various plants and herbs to enhance an individual’s feeling of well-being

 

arsenic a chemical element with a high degree of toxicity; subtoxic doses of arsenic are sometimes used in the treatment of cancer; a chemical that was once stockpiled by the United States for use as a chemical weapon

 

asepsis a state or condition in which a CSP remains free from pathogenic microorganisms

 

aseptic technique a set of practices and procedures used to minimize the potential introduction of contaminants (including infections) into CSPs

 

ASHP/ACPE Accreditation Standards for Pharmacy Technician Education and Training Programs the primary document for pharmacy technician programs seeking to achieve or maintain accreditation

 

ASHP’s Model Curriculum for Pharmacy Technician Education and Training Programs, Fifth Edition (2019) a set of standards created by a broad range of pharmacy stakeholders and published by the ASHP; the official curriculum used by ASHP/ACPE-accredited pharmacy technician education and training programs

 

aspiration a fine mist or aerosolized powder that may be expelled inadvertently into a PEC during sterile compounding procedures; an undesired consequence of incorrect sterile compounding technique

 

assessment of risk (AOR) an evaluation that determines if specific dosage forms of HDs may be handled using alternative containment practices or other work practices

 

assumption error a type of human-caused medication error that occurs when an essential piece of information cannot be verified; therefore, an assumption is made

 

attenuated virus a live virus that is scientifically manipulated to reduce its pathogenic nature so it may be used to vaccinate against a disease without causing the disease

 

autoclave a device that uses heat and/or steam under high pressure to sterilize supplies, surgical instruments, and certain drugs or CSPs

 

autoimmune disorder any disorder characterized by an immune response directed against the body’s own tissues

 

automated compounding device (ACD) a machine that houses pump stations, each of which is fitted with tubing (a lead); one end of each lead spikes into a container holding a PN component, and the other end merges into the centralized PN pump station tubing, which automatically delivers fluid into the final container; also known as an automated PN compounder; automix; or, simply, a compounder

 

Automated Compounding Device (ACD) Cleaning and Verification Log Sheet a written record of the cleaning and calibration of an ACD; this log sheet is located in the anteroom and must be kept on file within the pharmacy for the life of the equipment

 

automix compounder a type of automated PN compounding device manufactured and distributed by Baxter, a pharmaceutical company

 

autopsy a postmortem examination and dissection performed by a pathologist to determine an individual’s cause of death

 

auxiliary label a small label affixed to a primary CSP label; this label provides additional information or special instructions relating to the administration, handling, or storage of the sterile preparation as well as possible side effects or drug interactions related to the use of the medication

 

avoirdupois system of measurement a virtually obsolete system of measurement used predominantly by pharmacies prior to the metric system; originated in France

 

ayurvedic medicine a form of alternative medicine native to India; therapies are based on complex combinations of herbal substances and minerals as well as spirituality, diet, and lifestyle modifications

 

bacteria single-celled microorganisms that may cause a variety of illnesses and diseases and are easily transmitted to others by direct contact; characteristic bacterial shapes include spherical, rod-shaped, and spiral-shaped

 

bag-and-vial system a system in which an IVPB solution is attached to a parenteral drug vial by means of a specially manufactured adapter on the IVPB solution container; this container allows the IVPB bag to be screwed or snapped directly onto the vial for later activation by a nurse; see also addEASE, Add-A-Vial, ADD-Vantage, and MINI-BAG Plus

 

balance scale a device used since at least 2400 bce that measures powdered or other solid ingredients; used in some extemporaneous pharmacy compounds

 

barrel the inner part of a syringe into which fluid is drawn and held

 

base solution a sterile solution to which additives may be injected prior to IV administration

 

basic formula calculation a pharmaceutical dosage calculation method that uses the formula D / H = x mL; with the letter D representing the desired dose, the letter H representing the concentration on hand, and the letter x representing the unknown volume of drug needed to be drawn up for the preparation of a CSP

 

basic formula method a simple method to calculate most parenteral medication dosages; may be used when the concentration on the drug label is provided in units (usually milligrams per one milliliter [mg/mL]); based on the formula: D/H = x mL

 

batch the entire number of CSPs needed for all patients for a specific period; all of the CSPs for a 24-hour period that are often prepared by sterile compounding personnel during a single compounding period; also referred to as the IV run or IV pick

 

belladonna a potentially toxic substance used as a basis for some traditional and homeopathic medicines; also known as deadly nightshade

 

benign describes a growth of nonmalignant cells or a harmless medical condition

 

benzyl alcohol a solvent commonly used as a preservative in injectable medications

 

benzyl alcohol gasping syndrome a condition caused by the injection of a preparation containing benzyl alcohol into neonatal patients; may result in severe respiratory distress or death

 

Best Pharmaceuticals for Children Act a 2002 act that sought to improve the safety and efficacy of children’s medications by encouraging pediatric studies and drug trials

 

bevel the slanted opening at the tip of a needle

 

beyond-use date (BUD) the date and time after which a CSP must not be used

 

biological agent a type of medicinal product created using biological processes; includes vaccines, allergy treatments, and gene therapy

 

biological safety cabinet (BSC) a containment primary engineering control (C–PEC) designed for the preparation of HD CSPs; a BSC utilizes vertical airflow and other safeguards designed to protect the compounder from exposure to HDs

 

Black Plague a devastating pandemic thought to be spread by fleas on rats; estimated to have killed 50% of Europe’s population in the mid-14th century; also known as the Black Death

 

blastema theory a theory of cancer causation developed in the 19th century by the German pathologist Johannes Müller; this theory argued that cancer cells arose from “budding elements” (called blastema) in connective tissue

 

bloodletting a common medieval practice in which a practitioner would use a lancet to pierce the vein of an ill individual and, subsequently, drain the patient’s “bad blood” into a basin or container; practice was based on the commonly held belief that draining an individual’s blood would remove toxins from the body and balance the four humors

 

blower a component of a PEC or C-PEC that draws air into the cabinet, forces the air through the filter (thus creating an ISO Class 5 environment), and then pushes the air out of the cabinet through the exhaust HEPA filter

 

body surface area (BSA) an estimated measurement of the human body based on a patient’s height and weight and expressed in meters squared (m2), sometimes used to determine patient-specific dosing expressed in mg/m2

 

bone marrow suppression the decreased ability of the bone marrow to manufacture blood cells; a common side effect of chemotherapy

 

Bower Manuscript an ancient manuscript written by East Indian shaman Sushruta that lists 1,120 illnesses and the herbal medicines for treating them; manuscript that laid the foundation for ayurvedic medicine

 

break ring a ring of thin glass around the neck of an ampule that is intended to make breaking the ampule neck easier

 

Broviac line a tunneled central venous catheter that allows IV access; may also be called a Broviac catheter

 

BSC’s HEPA filter a filter that provides a source of clean air for the preparation of CSPs, thus establishing an ISO Class 5 environment within a BSC

 

buffer room an ISO Class 7 (or cleaner) room where PECs are physically located; may be referred to as the IV room or sterile compounding room

 

buffer solution a solution, such as sodium bicarbonate, that is sometimes injected into a CSP to neutralize the CSP’s acidity and, therefore, prevent patient discomfort during parenteral administration

 

bulk substances active pharmaceutical ingredients and other components needed to prepare a CSP when a manufacturer’s product does not exist

 

Bureau of Narcotics and Dangerous Drugs a US government agency created to oversee issues related to dangerous drugs; merged with the Federal Bureau of Narcotics to become the US Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA)

 

buretrol a type of tubing with a large chamber used to dilute and administer parenteral medications for pediatric IV therapy; also known as a chamber device

 

button injection port a type of injection port located on the face of certain IV base solution bags; also known as a nipple injection port

 

cachexia profound general ill health and malnutrition, typically accompanied by weakness, emaciation, and muscle wasting

 

calibration mark a graduation mark on the exterior surface of a supply item such as the barrel of a syringe, an LVP solution bag, or an IVPB solution bag that indicates fluid volume

 

cancer a class of diseases characterized by the presence of abnormal cells that divide and multiply uncontrollably and invade nearby tissues; the natural course of cancer is fatal

 

cannabidiol (CBD) the active ingredient in cannabis (marijuana)

 

Cannabis the plant source of marijuana, a Schedule I controlled substance

 

cannula a small, sterile tube or needle that may be used to gain or maintain access to a patient’s vein or artery for delivery of IV fluids or medications; may contain a sharp trocar for creating an entrance point into the vein; may also be referred to as a catheter

 

capillary a small blood vessel that connects arteries and veins

 

capture error a type of human-caused medication error that occurs when a more practiced behavior automatically takes the place of a less familiar, intended one

 

carbolic acid an organic compound and early antimicrobial agent derived from tar which was used to disinfect surgical instruments, incisions, and dressings; also known as phenol

 

carcinogenic giving rise to cancer; cancer-causing

 

carcinoma a cancerous tumor or malignant cell

 

cardioplegia solution a sterile, isotonic, crystalloid solution used to induce cardiac stasis and to protect the myocardium during open-heart surgery

 

Category 1 CSP a CSP that may be prepared in a PEC located in an unclassified SCA; has a BUD of 12 hours or less at controlled room temperature or 24 hours or less when refrigerated

 

Category 2 CSP a CSP that is prepared in a PEC located in a buffer room; has a BUD greater than 12 hours at controlled room temperature and greater than 24 hours when refrigerated, provided that the CSP is compounded within all of the parameters identified in USP <797>

 

caustic describes a chemical that is burning, corrosive, or destructive to living tissue; describes a characteristic of certain undiluted drugs when injected directly into muscles or veins

 

cellulitis an inflammation or infection of the tissues beneath the skin

 

Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) an agency that protects the United States from health, safety, and security threats; conducts research into the causes, treatments, and prevention of illnesses and diseases; and promotes healthy and safe behaviors for individuals

 

Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) a federal agency within the US Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) that administers Medicare (healthcare services for individuals over age 65) and Medicaid (healthcare services for lower-income families)

 

central line a central venous catheter placed into a large vein in the neck to provide IV access

 

central line–associated bloodstream infection (CLABSI) a serious infection caused by harmful microorganisms entering the bloodstream through a central line

 

central nervous system (CNS) depression a physiological degradation of the CNS that results in decreased autonomic functions, such as respirations and heart rate

 

central pharmacy the main or primary pharmacy that serves the needs of an entire hospital and provides backup support for the facility’s satellite or decentralized pharmacies

 

central venous catheter (CVC) a catheter inserted into a large vein, such as the internal jugular vein or the subclavian vein, and leading into the superior vena cava or right atrium; catheter used to administer medications or parenteral nutrition (PN); also called a central line

 

Certificate of Analysis (COA) a certificate that attests the quality or effectiveness of a test media or sterile device based on product specifications

 

certification the process by which a nongovernmental association grants recognition to an individual who has met certain predetermined qualifications specified by that profession

 

chelation therapy a drug therapy typically administered to patients who have been diagnosed with heavy metal poisoning from exposure or ingestion of lead, mercury, and arsenic; therapy involves the use of chelators, or medications that bind metals in the bloodstream

 

chemical incompatibility a type of incompatibility that occurs when two substances are mixed, producing a chemical change (e.g., decomposition or a destructive alteration to the pH of the solution)

 

chemical property the chemical nature of a parenteral solution—specifically, the solution’s pH value, tonicity, osmolarity, osmolality, and compatibility; may also be called a chemical characteristic

 

chemical sterilization a method of surface sterilization used on objects or equipment; the process of using an agent to kill microorganisms

 

chemotherapeutic antibiotic an antibiotic that is more toxic than traditional antibiotics because it has the ability to kill cancer cells by interacting with DNA; also called an antitumor antibiotic

 

chemotherapy the treatment of a disease, usually cancer, by chemical agents

 

chemotherapy dispensing pin a small plastic device with a spike at one end, a Luer-lock adapter at the other end, and—in between—a small vent to which a 0.22 micron HEPA filter is attached; this device is used to relieve the negative pressure within a drug vial safely, while its built-in HEPA filter traps any drug particles or escaping fluid from the vial

 

chemotherapy support order a medication order for various nonhazardous drugs intended to make chemotherapy easier or more tolerable for the patient; this order may include LVPs for hydration, antiemetics, and medications for pain or anxiety; also referred to as a chemotherapy comfort order

 

chlorhexidine gluconate an antimicrobial agent commonly used in the surgical scrub sponge/brush products utilized in hand-washing procedures

 

chlorine a corrosive chemical that is used as a surface disinfectant or germicidal agent

 

chloroform an early anesthetic that was administered by having an individual breathe through a towel saturated with the drug; a medication used to produce anesthesia prior to surgery

 

chloroxylenol a chlorine-based phenol derivative that is sometimes used as an antiseptic cleaning agent in pharmacy and presurgical hand-washing procedures

 

circulatory system the system by which blood is moved throughout the body; may also be called the cardiovascular system

 

Clark’s Rule an outdated formula once used to determine a pediatric dose; formula is weight in pounds multiplied by the adult dose and then divided by 150 to equal the pediatric dose

 

Class I BSC a vertical airflow biological safety cabinet (BSC) used in chemical manufacturing but unsuitable for chemotherapy preparation because it does not provide a HEPA-filtered, ISO Class 5 environment

 

Class II BSC a vertical airflow biological safety cabinet (BSC) used for chemotherapy and other HD preparation; Class II, Type A1 and Type A2 BSCs recirculate a percentage of the BSC airflow back into the HD buffer room after it has been filtered through the exhaust HEPA filter; a Class II, Type B BSC forces air through the exhaust HEPA filter; specifically, a Type B1 BSC sends most of the air from the BSC through a dedicated exhaust ventilation system to the outside environment, whereas a Type B2 BSC exhausts all of the air from within the BSC in this manner

 

Class III torsion prescription balance a two-pan, torsion-type balance scale commonly used in pharmacy practice; utilizes both internal and external weights; may only be used to weigh masses ≥120 mg and typically no more than 60 g or 120 g

 

clean air zone the region wherein the air that comes from a PEC’s HEPA filter proceeds uninterrupted until it reaches the inner edge of the outer six-inch zone; sometimes called the direct compounding area (DCA) or first air

 

cleaning agent an agent used to remove dirt and debris

 

cleanroom an early surgical room in which carbolic acid was used to disinfect microorganisms in the operating site

 

cleanroom suite the combined anteroom and buffer room that delineates the sterile compounding environment

 

clinical outcome the clinical monitoring of a patient’s response to drug therapy (e.g., drug levels within the blood)

 

clinical pharmacist a pharmacist who has specialized training in drug dosing, therapy, and pharmacokinetics

 

clinical pharmacology the study of the effects of drugs on the human body

 

closed-system container a type of sterile container; a sealed, noncollapsible container, such as a vial; a type of container in which air pressure must be equalized prior to the withdrawal of fluid

 

closed-system transfer device (CSTD) a small, disposable device that safely draws fluid from a vial into a syringe or injects fluid from a syringe into an IV or IVPB bag; this device protects the compounder from exposure to cytotoxic drugs during chemotherapy preparation

 

clyster an ancient, syringe-like device used to either insert fluid into the rectum for disease treatment or fluid restoration, or to remove body fluids such as pus or blood

 

cocaine a Schedule II controlled substance known to produce feelings of euphoria and increased stamina

 

Code of Ethics for Pharmacists a set of principles that guides pharmacists when making ethical decisions and when establishing and maintaining professional relationships with patients, healthcare colleagues, and society; formal version adopted by the American Pharmacists Association (APhA)

 

Code of Ethics for Pharmacy Technicians a set of principles that guides pharmacy technicians when making ethical decisions and when establishing and maintaining professional relationships with patients, healthcare colleagues, and society; formal version adopted by the American Association of Pharmacy Technicians (AAPT)

 

codeine an opium derivative; a Schedule II narcotic analgesic that may be classified as a Schedule III substance when combined with a nonnarcotic analgesic (such as acetaminophen) or a Schedule V substance when combined with a cough suppressant (such as dextromethorphan)

 

colectomy excision of part or all of the colon

 

colon hydrotherapy an alternative healing modality originally popularized in the early 20th century but still in use today; involves the flushing of the colon typically via a water tube or hose inserted into the rectum; also known as colon cleansing

 

colony-forming unit (CFU) the unit used to estimate colonies of viable bacteria or fungi on a media sample

 

combined form the form of a medical word element that is created when a root word is linked to a combining vowel

 

combining vowel a vowel (typically an o, but sometimes an i or e) added to the end of a root word that aids the pronunciation of the root word and its connection to a suffix

 

commercially available sterile product a sterile product that has been produced by pharmaceutical manufacturing facilities that adhere to Current Good Manufacturing Practices (GMPs) for product quality and consistency; also known as a conventionally manufactured sterile product or an off-the-shelf sterile product

 

compatibility the ability to combine two or more base components or additives within a solution without creating a resultant change in the physical or chemical properties of any of the solution components or additives

 

compounded sterile preparation (CSP) a preparation intended to be sterile that is created by combining, admixing, diluting, pooling, reconstituting, repackaging, or otherwise altering a drug product or bulk drug substance

 

compounded syringe a CSP that is prepared in a syringe final container so that it can be administered via a syringe pump or IVP route of administration

 

compounding aseptic containment isolator (CACI) a type of enclosed RABS that has an ISO Class 5 environment and is designed to protect sterile compounding personnel and the environment during the preparation of HDs

 

compounding aseptic isolator (CAI) a type of enclosed RABS that provides the required ISO Class 5 environment for the preparation of nonhazardous CSPs

 

Compounding Quality Act Title I of the DQSA, which identifies Section 503B and delineates the circumstances under which compounded medications can be prepared; see also Section 503A and Section 503B

 

Compounding Record a document that specifies details for each CSP or batch that is compounded

 

computerized provider order entry (CPOE) the process of entering medication and other treatment orders directly into a pharmacy-networked computer system by a physician or other prescriber; also known as computerized physician order entry or e-prescribing

 

concentrated form a sterile, injectable medication that has been reconstituted from a powdered form to a liquid form or that is supplied in a liquid form

 

concentration the strength of a medication; the amount or weight of a drug, ingredient, or solute in a given volume of solution; may be defined as percent strength (%), units/mL, mg/mL, mEq/mL, etc.

 

congenital disorder a physical condition present at birth

 

contact time the time a disinfectant needs to remain wet on a surface to ensure its effectiveness; also known as dwell time

 

contagion any transmittable infectious agent or contagious disease

 

containment primary engineering control (C–PEC) a ventilated PEC designed for the preparation of HDs because it protects the CSP, personnel, and the environment from contaminants; examples include a BSC and a CACI

 

containment secondary engineering control (C–SEC) the negative-pressure buffer room where the C–PEC is located; examples include an ISO Class 7 buffer room with 30 air changes per hour (ACPH) or an unclassified containment segregated compounding area (C–SCA) with 12 ACPH; both types of C–SECs must be externally vented to the outdoors and have a negative pressure between –0.01 and –0.03 inches of water column relative to surrounding areas

 

containment segregated compounding area (C–SCA) a type of Containment-Secondary Engineering Control (C-SEC) with nominal requirements for airflow and room pressurization as they pertain to HD compounding

 

contamination an unintended presence of a viable or nonviable substance

 

continual professional development (CPD) model a framework of lifelong learning that helps individuals to develop and maintain competence in professional skills and achieve career goals
