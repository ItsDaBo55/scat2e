Part 2: Training

7.7 Exploratory Lab

Now that you, as a sterile compounding student, have a basic understanding of the identification and verification of medication label information and a familiarity with several pharmacy calculation methods, you have the opportunity to practice these skills.

 

Specifically, this exploratory lab asks you to cross-check a medication order and CSP label, read a stock drug label for potassium chloride, and determine the volume of potassium chloride that must be drawn up into a syringe and injected into a Lactated Ringer’s solution with a total volume of 1,000 mL. In practice, once the volume of potassium chloride has been drawn up into a syringe and has been verified by a pharmacist, you will use aseptic technique to inject the medication into an IV bag. You will learn more about this part of the sterile compounding process in subsequent chapters of this textbook.

 

Note: Some of the steps ask you to read and respond to one or more multiple-choice questions (designated in orange type, such as 1–1 and 1–2). Record your answers to these multiple-choice questions by either circling the correct answers on the pages or recording and submitting your answers as directed by your instructor.

 

 

Supplies

  • sample medication order (Figure 7.15)
  • sample CSP label (Figure 7.16)
  • sample ingredient label (Figure 7.17)
  • basic calculator
  • scratch paper and pencil

 

 

Procedure

Take a careful, deliberate approach to each detailed step of this exploratory lab. In practice, using this approach ensures your understanding of the skills necessary for real-world sterile compounding.

 

 

Cross-Checking the Medication Order and the CSP Label

  1. Review the medication order (see Figure 7.15) to determine the patient’s name and room number (or location).
  2. Check the CSP label (see Figure 7.16) to verify that the spelling of the patient’s name and the patient’s room number match the medication order.
  3. Scan the medication order to find the following order: IVF: LR w/KCl 20 mEq/L @ 150 mL/hr × 3 L.
  4. Check the CSP label to verify that the label clearly indicates the drug name, strength, base solution name and volume, and infusion rate.

 

Figure 7.15 Medication Order: Pharmacy personnel must master the ability to read and interpret all aspects of a medication order to be able to accurately determine which items are dispensed by the pharmacy and which items require sterile compounding before being dispensed from the pharmacy. © Paradigm Education Solutions

 

Figure 7.16 CSP Label for Potassium Chloride (KCl) in Lactated Ringer’s: Potassium chloride in Lactated Ringer’s solution is a commonly used CSP that provides a patient with hydration and electrolytes. © Paradigm Education Solutions

 

  1. Answer the following multiple-choice questions, recording your answers as directed by your instructor.
     

5–1.  Looking at the medication order, what base solution has the prescriber ordered?

a. potassium chloride

b. Lactated Ringer’s

c. 20 mEq

d. 150 mL/hr


5–2.  Looking at the medication order, what is the volume of the base solution that the prescriber has ordered?

a. 150 mL

b. 10 L

c. 3 L

d. 1 L

 

5–3.  Looking at the CSP label, what is the name of the drug that the prescriber has ordered?

a. potassium chloride

b. Lactated Ringer’s

c. mEq

d. LVP

 

5–4.  Looking at the CSP label, what is the dose of the drug that the prescriber has ordered?

a. 150 mL

b. 1,000 mL

c. 1 L

d. 20 mEq

 

5–5.  Looking at the CSP label, what is the infusion rate that the prescriber has ordered?

a. 20 mEq per liter

b. Lactated Ringer’s 1,000 mL

c. 150 mL per hour

d. 20 mL per liter

 

 

Reading the Ingredient Label

  1. Carefully read the ingredient label, also known as the drug vial or medication label, to determine the concentration of the medication in the container (see Figure 7.17).

 

Figure 7.17 Ingredient Label for Potassium Chloride: Potassium chloride is a “high-alert” medication that is commonly used during sterile compounding. Personnel should take great care to perform calculations with accuracy as even relatively small errors in dosage of this medication could lead to serious injury or death for a patient recipient. © Paradigm Education Solutions

 

  1. Answer the following multiple-choice questions, recording your answers as directed by your instructor.

 

7–1.  Looking at the ingredient label, what is the name of the medication in the vial?

a. EMCP

b. Lactated Ringer’s

c. potassium chloride

d. injection concentrate, USP

 

7–2.  Looking at the ingredient label, what is the concentration of the medication in the vial?

a. 2 mEq/mL

b. 20 mL

c. 40 mEq

d. 20 mEq

 

 

Determining Which Calculation Method to Use

  1. Review the criteria (provided earlier in this chapter) for using the following calculation methods: basic formula, ratio and proportion, dimensional analysis, IV flow rate, IV drip rate, and alligation.
  2. Use the information provided on the CSP label and the medication label—along with the criteria for the various calculation methods—to determine which method is most appropriate for determining how much potassium chloride must be drawn up for this dose.
  3. Answer the following multiple-choice question, recording your answer as directed by your instructor.

 

10–1.  Based on the criteria identified for each of the calculation methods, which calculation method is most appropriate for determining the volume of potassium chloride that must be drawn up to deliver a 20 mEq dose?

a. ratio and proportion

b. dimensional analysis

c. IV drip rate

d. basic formula

 

 

Performing the Calculation

  1. Once you have determined which method you will use, write the equation on a piece of paper.
  2. Use a calculator to perform the calculation(s).
  3. Answer the following multiple-choice questions, recording your answers as directed by your instructor.

 

13–1. Based on the ingredient label, what is the concentration of potassium chloride on hand?

a. 2 mEq/mL

b. 20 mEq/mL

c. 4 mEq/mL

d. 40 mEq/mL

 

13–2. Based on the calculation that you performed in 13–1, what is the volume of potassium chloride that must be drawn up to prepare a CSP that delivers 20 mEq of potassium chloride?

a. 40 mL

b. 30 mL

c. 20 mL

d. 10 mL
