Review and Assessment

Chapter Summary

  • Scientific discoveries of Louis Pasteur and Joseph Lister regarding germ transmission led to an increased awareness of the sterile compounding environment.
  • Hospital surgical rooms were the first areas known as cleanrooms.
  • The modern-day cleanroom arose from the need to manufacture radar and sonar components in contaminant-free areas.
  • The HEPA filter was invented during the Manhattan Project to protect scientists from radioactivity.
  • The US military developed early guidelines for the design of cleanrooms.
  • Industrial cleanrooms are regulated by standards set by the US Department of Public Health, the Nuclear Regulatory Commission, the National Aeronautics and Space Administration, and the National Institute of Standards and Technology.
  • Personnel prepare biological agents or pharmaceuticals in a laboratory cleanroom.
  • A cleanroom suite in a compounding pharmacy is one area where CSPs are prepared by sterile compounding personnel.
  • A cleanroom suite is a secondary engineering control or SEC.
  • A workbench or hood where CSPs are prepared is a primary engineering control or PEC.
  • USP sets standards for the design, setup, cleaning, and maintenance of the cleanroom suite; these standards are referred to as USP <797>.
  • USP <797> permits two types of environments for sterile compounding: a cleanroom suite or an SCA.
  • Only furniture, equipment, and supplies necessary for compounding are permitted in a cleanroom suite or SCA.
  • A cleanroom suite is a combined anteroom and buffer room and should be maintained at a temperature of 68° F (20° C) or cooler with a relative humidity at or below 60%.
  • An anteroom is a preparatory area where sterile compounding personnel perform hand hygiene, don garb, gather supplies, and perform pharmacy calculations.
  • The air in an ISO Class 8 environment like the anteroom is exchanged at least 20 times per hour, or 20 ACPH.
  • A buffer room may house several types of ISO Class 5 PECs that provide an appropriate environment for sterile compounding.
  • USP <797> requires compounding facilities to identify a designated person(s) who is responsible and accountable for the performance and operation of the facility and personnel in the preparation of CSPs and for other activities described in the standard.
  • The air in the buffer room is exchanged at least 30 times per hour, or 30 ACPH.
  • An SCA is an unclassified pharmacy area with a visibly defined perimeter and may only be used for the preparation of Category 1 CSPs.
  • SCA surfaces should be smooth, impervious, free from cracks and crevices, and nonshedding so they may be easily cleaned and disinfected.
  • PECs in the sterile compounding environment may be categorized in two ways: a laminar airflow system (LAFS) and a restricted-access barrier system (RABS).
  • Types of LAFSs include LAFWs, IVLFZs, and BSCs.
  • Types of RABS include CAIs and CACIs.
  • A pharmaceutical isolator is different from a RABS because it has a built-in decontamination system.
  • PECs used for HD compounding are called C–PECs.
  • BSCs and CACIs are types of C–PECs.
  • USP <797> establishes the air quality standards that are appropriate for sterile compounding as well as identifies several ISO classes that designate the amount of particulate matter in the air.
  • ISO classes are numbered from ISO Class 3 to ISO Class 8, with a higher number designating a greater allowance for particulate matter.
  • Cleaning agents are used to remove dirt and debris; disinfectants are used to destroy viruses, bacteria, and fungi; and sporicidal agents are used to kill bacterial and fungal spores.
  • Surfaces must be cleaned before they are disinfected unless a one-step, disinfectant cleaner is used.
  • Cleaning and disinfection procedures must be performed on established schedules by personnel who have been specifically trained in USP <797> standards, cleanroom suite procedures, and the proper use of cleaning and disinfection agents.
  • Environmental monitoring includes monitoring temperature, humidity, and pressure readings; this monitoring also involves viable surface and airborne particle testing in ISO-classified areas.
  • Certification personnel from a company that independently certifies sterile compounding equipment and cleanroom suites conduct air quality, circulation, velocity, and pressure tests at sterile compounding facilities.
  • USP <797> categorizes CSPs based on compounding environment, potential risk of microbial growth, storage, and the period within which a CSP must be used.
  • Category 1 CSPs are compounded in a PEC located in a SCA and must be assigned a BUD of 12 hours or less at controlled room temperature or 24 hours or less when refrigerated.
  • Category 2 CSPs must be compounded in a PEC located in a cleanroom suite but may have longer BUDs depending on testing performed.
  • Category 3 CSPs may have the longest BUDs but require higher quality assurance parameters than Category 2.
  • Personnel are the number one cause of contamination in the sterile compounding environment.
  • Equipment and supplies must be staged before they are brought into sterile compounding areas and again before they are brought into a PEC (unless they are sealed in sterile packages).
  • Compounding personnel must use aseptic technique when preparing CSPs.
  • Compounding must occur in the DCA of a PEC, and critical sites must be continuously exposed to unidirectional first air that has come through the PEC’s HEPA filter.
  • Sterile gloves must be wiped with sterile 70% IPA throughout the compounding process.
  • Shadowing occurs when first air is blocked by people or objects that come between the PEC’s HEPA filter and a critical site of a supply item.
  • Touch contamination can be caused from personnel touching a critical site of a supply item or from a critical site touching a contaminated surface.
  • CSPs must undergo release testing before being dispensed to patients; this testing involves inspection of CSPs for particulate matter or signs of incompatibility and may also involve testing for pH, sterility, and endotoxins.
