PART 1: CONCEPTS

4.5 Cleaning and Disinfection of the Sterile Compounding Environment

Surfaces in a sterile compounding environment must be cleaned and disinfected at specified intervals to eliminate potential sources of contamination. Cleaning agents are used to remove dirt and debris; disinfectants are used to destroy viruses, bacteria, and fungi; and sporicidal agents are used to kill bacterial and fungal spores.

 

USP <797> provides clear instructions for cleaning SECs (anteroom and buffer room or SCA). Every surface must be cleaned to remove dirt, debris, and other organic and inorganic materials.

 

 

Cleaning and Disinfection Supplies

The exact solutions used for the cleaning and disinfection of nonporous surfaces in the cleanroom suite are selected by pharmacy personnel based on their effectiveness, compatibility with the surfaces to which they are being applied, compatibility with other solutions they may contact, and the safety of personnel who use them. The types and use of cleaning and disinfection solutions are addressed in USP General Chapter <1072> Disinfectants and Antiseptics.

 

Name Exchange

The contact requirements of cleaning and disinfecting agents may be referred to as dwell time, minimum contact time, or, simply, contact time.

Whereas some facilities use separate cleaning and disinfecting agents, other facilities choose to use a one-step, disinfectant cleaner registered with the US Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) or an equivalent, which means that the agent is formulated to clean and disinfect in one step. Personnel should consult the manufacturer’s label of each product to determine the solution’s dwell time, or the period in which a solution must dwell or remain wet on a surface prior to its activation to kill microorganisms. This contact time typically ranges from 1–10 minutes to allow a solution to have the maximum effectiveness in disinfection.

 

It is considered best practice to utilize a single-use, low-lint wiper that is saturated with an appropriate, one-step, disinfectant cleaner. The wiper may be supplied by the manufacturer in a container that maintains product cleanliness and integrity. Reusable cleaning supplies such as nonwooden mop handles and buckets should be designated for use in the anteroom and buffer room or SCA only and should never be used to clean other pharmacy areas. These items must be cleaned and disinfected before and after each use and must not be removed from their dedicated storage area in the anteroom or SCA until disposal. Noncotton, low-lint mopheads should be disposable and must be discarded in a manner that minimizes dispersion of particles into the air.

 

 

Cleaning and Disinfection Process of SECs

Sterile compounding personnel must clean and disinfect work surfaces, pass-throughs, sinks, and floors of SECs daily. They should also perform cleaning and disinfection of these areas if they are visibly soiled, have been subject to spills, or have known or suspected contamination. If sterile compounding is not performed daily in a facility, personnel must clean and disinfect before initiating compounding procedures. All other surfaces including ceiling, walls, doors, doorframes, storage shelving, and storage bins must be cleaned and disinfected monthly using an agent with sporicidal activity and should also be cleaned and disinfected when visibly soiled and when contamination is known or suspected. (See Table 4.2 for a summary of this schedule.)

 

TABLE 4.2 Cleaning and Disinfection Schedules of SECs
Area of Sterile Compounding Environment Cleaning Schedule Disinfection Schedule* Sporicidal Schedule
Sink(s) Daily Daily Monthly
Pass-through(s) Daily Daily Monthly
Work surfaces outside a PEC Daily Daily Monthly
Floor(s) Daily Daily Monthly
Wall(s), door(s), and doorframes Monthly Monthly Monthly
Ceilings Monthly Monthly Monthly
Storage shelving and bins Monthly Monthly Monthly

*Many disinfectants registered by the EPA are one-step cleaning and disinfecting agents, which means that the disinfectant has been formulated to be effective in the presence of light-to-moderate soiling without a separate cleaning step.

 

Cleaning and disinfection procedures must be performed by personnel who have been specifically trained in USP <797> standards, cleanroom suite procedures, and the proper use of cleaning and disinfection agents. Personnel must demonstrate that they are properly trained and must undergo retraining annually and if there is any change in process, equipment, or supplies.

 

The cleaning and disinfection process begins in the cleanest area of the cleanroom suite and proceeds to the dirtiest area. For that reason, appropriately garbed personnel clean and disinfect the buffer room first, followed by the anteroom. Personnel should start the cleaning and disinfection process at the far end of the buffer room and move toward the door to the anteroom. They should begin with the ceiling, followed by the walls and the floor, and they should use overlapping strokes. This process should be repeated in the anteroom and then out toward the unclassified pharmacy area.

 

Safety Alert

To determine what protection is needed when working with cleaning and disinfection products, personnel should refer to their facility’s SOPs manual as well as the manufacturers’ instructions for use.

Personnel should also carefully read manufacturers’ instructions regarding the use of cleaning and disinfection agents. Some agents are highly concentrated and require dilution by personnel. Other agents require personnel to wear special protective equipment such as goggles and respirator masks.

 

At the completion of the cleaning and disinfection process, personnel must document their activities using designated cleaning logs. These records must be maintained in the pharmacy and must be furnished for agencies such as a state board of pharmacy, The Joint Commission (TJC), and the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA).

Cleaning and Disinfection of PECs

USP <797>

Cleaning and disinfection procedures for PECs may vary. For that reason, sterile compounding personnel should consult the manufacturers’ handbooks for the PECs regarding any special procedures.

Like the cleaning and disinfection of SECs, USP <797> provides clear instructions for the cleaning and disinfection of PECs. Sterile compounding personnel must clean and disinfect all interior surfaces of a PEC, including its ceiling, walls, hang bar and hooks, work surface, and any equipment inside the PEC. (See Table 4.3 for a summary of this schedule.)

 

TABLE 4.3 Cleaning and Disinfection Schedules of PECs
Component of PEC or Equipment inside PEC Cleaning Schedule Disinfection Schedule Sporicidal Schedule
Horizontal work surface
  • Daily
  • When contamination is known or suspected
  • Daily
  • When contamination is known or suspected
  • Apply sterile 70% IPA every 30 minutes during compounding periods (do not interrupt the compounding process); if the compounding process takes longer than 30 minutes, disinfect immediately after completion of the compounding process
  • Monthly
Ceiling, hang bar, hooks, walls
  • Daily
  • When contamination is known or suspected
  • Daily
  • When contamination is known or suspected
  • Monthly
Equipment
  • Daily
  • When contamination is known or suspected
  • Daily
  • When contamination is known or suspected
  • Monthly

 

This cleaning and disinfection process must occur daily when compounding is performed and when contamination is known or suspected. Personnel must also apply sterile 70% IPA to the horizontal work surface of a PEC at least every 30 minutes during continuous compounding periods. If the compounding process takes more than 30 minutes, personnel must immediately disinfect the work surface of the PEC after completion of compounding procedures.

 

In addition to this daily cleaning and disinfection process, sterile compounding personnel must use a sporicidal agent on the inside of a PEC and on all surfaces outside of a PEC on a monthly schedule. A sporicidal agent kills bacterial and fungal spores. (For additional information regarding the cleaning and disinfection of PECs, refer to Chapter 9.)
