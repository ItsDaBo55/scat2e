PART 1: CONCEPTS

4.4 Air Quality Standards in the Sterile Compounding Environment

USP <797>

An anteroom must be ISO Class 8 or better. A buffer room must be ISO Class 7 or better.

USP <797> identifies the air quality standards that are appropriate for the sterile compounding environment. In this environment, the term air quality refers to the level of airborne contaminants and the systems and processes that sterile compounding personnel must employ to protect CSPs from these contaminants.

 

 

ISO Standards

Practice Tip

The lower the ISO class number, the better the air quality.

The International Organization for Standardization (ISO) creates worldwide industrial and commercial standards that often become laws or are used in setting standards for accreditation, certification, or credentialing requirements. USP <797> identifies several ISO classes that designate the amount of particulate matter in room air (see Table 4.1). These classes are numbered from ISO Class 3 to ISO Class 8, with a higher number designating a greater allowance for particulate matter. The most restrictive category is ISO Class 3. This class designates the lowest allowance for particulate matter and, therefore, is the cleanest air. The least restrictive category is ISO Class 8. This class designates the highest allowance for particulate matter and, therefore, is the dirtiest air (see Table 4.1).

 

TABLE 4.1 ISO Classifications of Particulate Matter in Room Air*
ISO Class Maximum Particle Count per Cubic Meter of Air (of particles 0.5 micron or larger)†
3 35.2
4 352
5 3,520
6 35,200
7 352,000
8 3,520,000

*Adapted from ISO 14644-1, Cleanrooms and associated controlled environments—Part 1: Classification of air cleanliness by particle concentration

†Limits for number of particles ≥0.5 μm measured under dynamic operating conditions

 

USP <797>

When designating classification, USP <797> uses parameters identified in ISO 14644-1 Cleanrooms and Associated Control Environments; Part 1: Classification of air cleanliness by particle concentration.

In general, ISO class stringency within the pharmacy environment increases as sterile compounding personnel travel from an unclassified pharmacy area into an anteroom, then into a buffer room, and, finally, into a PEC. In general, the particulate matter contained in regular room air within a pharmacy is greater than ISO Class 8, or approximately 3,520,000 particles of 0.5 micron or larger per cubic meter of air. At the next level of stringency, the particulate matter in an anteroom must be no greater than ISO Class 8. Stricter still, the particulate matter in a buffer room must be no greater than ISO Class 7, or 352,000 particles of 0.5 micron or larger per cubic meter of air. This ISO Class 7 environment is created through the combined use of HEPA-filtered, positive-pressure air delivered through a designated HVAC system; strategic placement of vents; and nonporous construction components. In addition, trained personnel sustain the ISO Class 7 environment by performing specific procedures that maintain the cleanliness of the environment.

 

The air within the DCA of a PEC must be at least ISO Class 5, or no greater than 3,520 particles of 0.5 micron or larger per cubic meter of air. The ISO Class 5 environment within a PEC is created by pushing air through a HEPA filter on the back or the top of the PEC. (Chapter 9 of this textbook discusses the components and function of each part of a PEC in detail.)
