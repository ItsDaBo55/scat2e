9.4 Understand the Resources and Supplies

USP <797> standards prescribe requirements for cleaning and disinfecting PECs used in sterile compounding. This chapter and the corresponding lab provide resources and supplies that are in alignment with these standards and that are specific to cleaning a horizontal LAFW located in the buffer room of a cleanroom suite.

 

 

Essential Supplies

USP <797>

Sterile compounding personnel must don appropriate garb including sterile, powder-free, disposable gloves before pre­paring CSPs in an ISO Class 5 environment. With that in mind, all chapter references to “sterile gloves” indicate gloves with these characteristics.

Most sterile compounding procedures require the same essential supply items to be available for use in both an anteroom and a buffer room. For the anteroom, these supplies include disposable foot and head garb that consists of low-lint shoe covers; low-lint head covers and (if needed) facial hair covers; face masks; and reusable goggles when eye protection is needed. Hand hygiene supplies include soap in a nonrefillable container; disposable nail cleaners; low-lint, disposable towels or wipers; an alcohol-based hand rub; and sterile 70% IPA. Other garbing supplies include low-lint garments (gowns or coveralls) with sleeves that fit snugly around the wrists and that are enclosed at the neck and sterile, powder-free, disposable gloves.

 

For the buffer room, these supplies include sterile 70% IPA to wipe sterile gloves and to use for procedure-specific purposes during sterile compounding. Depending on a facility’s standard operating procedures (SOPs), sterile compounding personnel may perform the following procedures in the buffer room rather than in the anteroom: hand sanitizing using an alcohol-based hand rub and the donning of sterile gloves. If so, these essential supply items must be available for use in the buffer room.

 

 

Procedure-Specific Supplies

In addition to the previously listed essential supplies, each sterile compounding procedure requires supply items specific to the procedure being performed. The type, number, and amount of procedure-specific supply items are identified by sterile compounding personnel prior to the procedure.

 

Sterile compounding personnel should be aware that USP <797> mandates that they must wipe supply items before bringing them into the clean side of the anteroom (unless package integrity would be damaged). Wearing sterile gloves and using low-lint wipers, personnel must wipe these items with a sporicidal agent, a disinfectant registered with the US Environmental Protection Agency (EPA), or sterile 70% IPA. They must then wipe these supplies a second time using sterile 70% IPA before introducing them into a PEC. The only exception to these preparatory procedures is a supply item that has an outer wrapper that maintains its sterility.

 

 

PEC Cleaning Supplies

USP <797>

Disposable towels and wipers used in the sterile compounding area must be low-lint to reduce the potential for particle shedding.

The proper cleaning and disinfection of a PEC require the use of appropriate agents and related supplies, the execution of the correct process, and the completion of required documentation. Therefore, a familiarity with the supply items and their specific handling requirements is essential to sterile compounding procedures.

 

 

Low-Lint Wipers

Best Practice

Sterile compounding personnel should avoid using spray bottles within a PEC, as the fluid aspirate may damage the HEPA filter. If a spray bottle is the only container type available, personnel should unscrew the top and tip the bottle to saturate the sterile, low-lint wipers as they lay on the PEC’s work surface. When finished, personnel should replace the top onto the bottle.

As mentioned earlier in this chapter, sterile compounding personnel must use low-lint wipers when applying cleaning and disinfecting agents. Low-lint wipers used within the PEC must be sterile. The wipers should be kept inside a protective container. If sterile wipers are used, they should be kept inside their original container and opened within the DCA of a PEC.

 

Personnel should use a new wiper to clean and disinfect each section of a PEC. Because the outer six-inch zone of a PEC is an area where the HEPA-filtered air mixes with room air, this area is considered contaminated. Therefore, as each wiper is brought to the outer edge of the PEC, it becomes contaminated and must be discarded into the waste container.

 

A sterile compounding technician records the BUD on an opened bottle of sterile water. George Brainard/© Paradigm Education Solutions

 

Sterile compounding personnel should be aware that some facilities allow the use of a new side of the wiper for the cleaning and disinfection of each surface. If so, personnel may, for example, fold a large wiper in fourths and then wipe each section of the PEC using an unused surface of the wiper. Personnel should check their facility’s SOPs for guidance.

 

 

Sterile Water

USP <797>

Depending on a facility’s SOPs, personnel should record the BUD on the label of an opened container of sterile water. They should also designate on the container that its contents only be used for PEC cleaning purposes.

To begin the PEC cleaning process, sterile compounding personnel should use sterile, low-lint wipers to remove visible residue that may not be soluble to sterile 70% IPA. The sterile water is typically supplied in a 250 mL (or larger) pour bottle that can be recapped between PEC cleanings.

 

Sterile compounding personnel should pour the sterile water onto the sterile, low-lint wipers and then wipe areas of the PEC that have visible residue. When the task is completed, personnel should record the beyond-use date (BUD) on the container’s label, depending on their facility’s SOPs. This documentation is important because sterile water contains no preservatives; therefore, any remaining fluid in the bottle must be discarded by the BUD.

 

 

PEC Cleaning and Disinfecting Agents

After sterile compounding personnel have removed visible residue using sterile water, they must apply a cleaning agent followed by a disinfectant, unless an EPA-registered, one-step disinfectant cleaner is used. EPA-registered disinfectant cleaners have both cleaning and disinfecting properties within a single product. To be effective in killing viruses, bacteria, and fungi, the disinfecting agent must dwell on the surfaces of a PEC for the period listed on the manufacturer’s product label. This dwell time is referred to as contact time. Personnel must also apply a disinfectant with activity against bacterial and fungal spores at least monthly. These disinfectants are commonly known as sporicidal agents.

 

 

Sterile 70% IPA

After cleaning and disinfecting the surfaces of a PEC, sterile compounding personnel must use a sterile, low-lint wiper to apply sterile 70% IPA to remove any residue from the use of these cleaning and disinfecting agents. Personnel must then allow the sterile 70% IPA to dry before sterile compounding procedures can begin.

 

Workplace Wisdom

Sterile compounding personnel should know the forms, uses, and handling of sterile 70% IPA.

 

Forms Uses Handling

Spray bottle of sterile 70% IPA; typically packaged in a solvent-safe, double-bagged overwrap for easy transfer into a buffer room

 Applied to sterile, low-lint wipers; used to wipe surfaces of a cleanroom suite and a PEC; also used to wipe the exterior surfaces of supplies and sterile gloves.
  • Personnel must inspect bottle to ensure its integrity (no signs of damage from shipping or storage).
  • Personnel who are using the spray bottle within a PEC should unscrew the spray nozzle, remove the nozzle, and then pour the fluid onto the sterile, low-lint wipers to saturate them.   • Personnel should record on the opened bottle’s label the BUD and use of the fluid within the manufacturer’s recommendation or according to their facility’s SOPs.

PEC Cleaning and Disinfection Documentation

A facility’s SOPs dictate how and when cleaning activities, including the cleaning and disinfection of PECs, are documented. A PEC cleaning and disinfection checklist should be kept inside of a plastic sheet protector to prevent excess shedding of particles from the paper. The checklist typically contains columns for the cleaning solution, disinfection solution, date, time, and the signature or initials of the individual who has performed the PEC cleaning and disinfection procedures (see Figure 9.2). During a work shift, sterile compounding personnel should repeatedly perform this task. Each time personnel perform PEC cleaning and disinfection, they should complete the checklist assigned to that PEC. Personnel should be aware that this checklist is an official document that may be reviewed by regulatory and compliance organizations, such as a state’s board of pharmacy or an accreditation agency, such as The Joint Commission.
