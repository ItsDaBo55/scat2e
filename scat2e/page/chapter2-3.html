PART 1: CONCEPTS

2.3 Medication Safety Resources

The ISMP and TJC have developed medication safety resources to make healthcare personnel aware of the potential dangers of handling certain medications. These resources include compilations of high-alert medications, look-alike and sound-alike drugs, and medical abbreviations to avoid in healthcare communications.

 

 

ISMP Resources

To alert healthcare personnel to the potential dangers of handling and administering certain medications, the ISMP has created several valuable resources. One resource is a list of high-alert medications that bear a heightened risk of causing significant patient harm when used in error. These medications include potassium chloride, paralytic medications, chemotherapy agents, digoxin, amiodarone, and warfarin, to name a few. The ISMP’s high-alert medications are divided into three lists: List of High-Alert Medications in Acute-Care Settings, List of High-Alert Medications in Community /Ambulatory Healthcare Settings, and List of High-Alert Medications in Long-Term Care (LTC) Settings. These lists are periodically updated by the ISMP and can be accessed online at the following location: https://SterileComp2.ParadigmEducation.com/HighAlertMeds. High-alert drugs are designated with auxiliary labels that alert healthcare personnel to their potential risks (see Figure 2.4).

 

 

FIGURE 2.4 High-Alert Auxiliary Labels: Medications that carry a high potential for patient danger if dosed or administered improperly often have high-alert auxiliary labels affixed to their containers. © Paradigm Education Solutions

 

In addition, the ISMP, in coordination with the FDA, publishes a list of common look-alike and sound-alike drugs that often contribute to medication errors. This list, known as the List of Confused Drug Names, offers hundreds of drug names that are easily confused—for example, DAUNOrubicin and DOXOrubicin. The resource can be accessed online at the following location: https://SterileComp2.ParadigmEducation.com/ConfusedDrugNames. To view a separate list of just look-alike drugs with recommended tall-man lettering, refer to the FDA and ISMP Lists of Look-Alike Drug Names with Recommended Tall Man Letters at the following location: https://SterileComp2.ParadigmEducation.com/ISMPTallManLetters.

 

The ISMP also publishes the List of Error-Prone Abbreviations to help healthcare personnel avoid dangerous documentation. The abbreviations and other shorthand communication methods on this list have been identified as the cause of many medication errors and, therefore, should never be used in healthcare practice. This list can be accessed online at the following location: https://SterileComp2.ParadigmEducation.com/ErrorProneList.

 

Finally, the ISMP publishes the ISMP Guidelines for Safe Electronic Communication of Medication Information. These guidelines address the use of accurate documentation standards in electronic healthcare communication channels such as electronic health records (EHRs), electronic prescribing (e-prescribing), CPOE systems, and other types of pharmacy automated systems. This publication can be accessed online at the following location: https://SterileComp2.ParadigmEducation.com/ElectronicGuidelines. (For more information on the ISMP Guidelines, see Chapter 6.)

 

 

TJC Resources

As mentioned earlier, TJC accreditation of a hospital is dependent on demonstrating that a medication error reporting system is in place. For that reason, TJC works with healthcare facilities to improve their medication safety practices. In support of the ISMP, TJC recommends the elimination of error-prone abbreviations and the education of healthcare personnel regarding frequently confused drug names. The organization publishes the Official “Do Not Use” List of Abbreviations. This list can be accessed online at the following location: https://SterileComp2.ParadigmEducation.com/DNUfacts.
