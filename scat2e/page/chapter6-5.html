PART 2: TRAINING

6.5 Exploratory Lab

Now that you, as a sterile compounding student, have a basic understanding of how to identify and interpret the different components of medication orders and labels that you will typically encounter in a sterile compounding facility, you have the opportunity to visualize and explore these items.

 

Note: Some of the steps ask you to read and respond to one or more multiple-choice questions (designated in orange type, such as 1.1 and 1.2). Record your answers to these multiple-choice questions by either circling the correct answers on the pages or recording and submitting your answers as directed by your instructor.

 

 

Supplies

  • admission order for Hayashi (Figure 6.1)
  • daily order for Echeverria (Figure 6.2)
  • standing order for Cruz (Figure 6.3)
  • IVPB label for Andreachi (Figure 6.4)
  • large-volume parenteral label for Li (Figure 6.5)
  • syringe label for Decoteau (Figure 6.6)
  • sheet of notebook paper and a pen

 

 

Procedure

Take a careful, deliberate approach to each detailed step of this exploratory lab. In practice, using this approach ensures your understanding of the skills necessary for real-world sterile compounding.

 

Study Idea

Appendix A offers a list of commonly used medical and pharmacy abbreviations, acronyms, and symbols used in the medication orders referenced in this Exploratory Lab.

 

 

Admission Order for Hayashi

  1. Refer back to Figure 6.1 in this chapter and read the admission order for Hayashi in its entirety.
  2. Answer the following multiple-choice question, recording your answer as directed by your instructor.

     

    2–1. According to the medication order, what are the patient’s allergies?

    1. no known allergy
    2. sodium, potassium, and Demerol
    3. no known drug allergy
    4. sodium and potassium chloride

     

  3. Find the circled number 5 on the order.
  4. Answer the following multiple-choice question, recording your answer as directed by your instructor.

     

    4–1. Which list of abbreviations and their corresponding meanings is reflected in the admission order?

    1. IV (intravenous), HL (heparin lock)
    2. IV (four), HL (heparin lock)
    3. IV (intravenous), HL (half-liter)
    4. IV (four), HL (half-liter)

     

  5. Find the heading “Meds” on the order. Scroll down to the sixth medication on the list.
  6. Answer the following multiple-choice question, recording your answer as directed by your instructor.

     

    6–1. What is meant by the order “LOC prn”?

    1. treat for loss of consciousness as needed
    2. laxative of choice in the evening
    3. laxative of choice as needed
    4. treat for loss of consciousness in the evening

Standing Order for Cruz

    2. 67 inches, 217 pounds, 05/11/55

    4. 5 feet and 6 inches, 217 pounds, 05/11/55

    3. Ritalin Long-Acting to be infused at 90 mg/hour
