Review and Assessment

Chapter Summary

  • Prior to the 19th century, healthcare practices were guided by local individuals including shamans, healers, alchemists, herbalists, apothecaries, and physicians.
  • During the Industrial Revolution, US citizens relied mainly on imported, adulterated drugs from England, which were freely distributed due to an unstructured healthcare system and the absence of legislative controls.
  • The Drug Importation Act of 1848 restricted the importation of adulterated drugs by hiring inspectors at ports of entry.
  • Citizens became the victims of the un-ethical practices of small-time hucksters who hawked miracle drugs and bogus vaccines as part of traveling medicine shows.
  • The Vaccine Act of 1813 was the first federal law addressing consumer protection and pharmaceutical production and was implemented to ensure the quality of vaccines.
  • In 1821, the Philadelphia College of Pharmacy became the first college of pharmacy in the United States.
  • The American Pharmacists Association (APhA) was created in 1852 and is the largest association of pharmacists in the United States.
  • The Pure Food & Drugs Act was established in 1906 and set standards for both the food and drug industries.
  • The Sherley Amendment was enacted in 1912 to prohibit “false and fraudulent” therapeutic claims on medicine labels.
  • The Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic (FD&C) Act was passed in 1938 and required drug manufacturers to perform animal testing on all new drugs, dictated that pharmaceutical manufacturers file a New Drug Application (NDA) before FDA approval, recognized the USP–NF as the official compendium of drug standards, authorized inspections of pharmaceutical facilities, and provided oversight of manufacturing practices.
  • Contamination of sulfathiazole led to the establishment of CGMPs, which mandate that pharmaceutical products are manufactured according to QA standards.
  • The Durham-Humphrey Amendment of 1951 was an amendment to the 1938 FD&C Act that categorized drugs into legend (prescription) drugs and over-the-counter (OTC) drugs.
  • Deformities caused by the use of thalidomide during pregnancy spurred the passage of the Kefauver-Harris Amendment in 1962. This legislation was an amendment to the FD&C Act and required that drugs undergo clinical trials to prove their safety and efficacy prior to marketing. 
  • The Drug Efficacy Study Implementation (DESI) evaluated more than 3,000 products and found that about one-third of the drugs were not effective based on their therapeutic claims. There are still a number of these drugs on the market that are not FDA approved.
  • Tamper-Resistant Packaging Requirements established in 1982 required certain products to be sold in tamper-resistant packaging, and the Federal Anti-Tampering Act of 1983 made it a federal crime to tamper with packaged consumer products.
  • The Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) was passed in 1996 to place safeguards on patients’ protected health information (PHI). HIPAA placed limits on how much of a patient’s PHI may be shared.
  • The Public Health Security and Bioterrorism Preparedness and Response Act of 2002 bolstered the ability of the United States to prevent, prepare for, and respond to acts of bioterrorism and other public health emergencies using public health surveillance and reporting systems and trained and prepared medical personnel.
  • The Best Pharmaceuticals for Children Act of 2002 sought to improve the safety and efficacy of children’s medications.
  • The Drug Quality and Security Act (DQSA) passed in 2013 was an amendment to the FD&C Act. Title I of the DQSA, the Compounding Quality Act,  identified 503B. Title II of the DQSA established the Drug Supply Chain Security Act (DSCSA), which established requirements related to the tracking of prescription drug products throughout the supply chain. This section of the DQSA is commonly referred to as Track and Trace.
  • The Agriculture Improvement Act (the 2018 Farm Bill) removed hemp with a THC concentration of not more than 0.3 percent on a dry weight basis as a controlled substance under the CSA.
  • Federal regulatory agencies provide oversight and enforcement of federal laws and regulations.
  • US Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) oversees the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) and houses the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) as well as many other agencies.
  • The FDA may inspect a sterile compounding facility to ensure it complies with Section 503A of the FD&C. 
  • Section 503A limits compounding to an identified individual patient based on the receipt of a valid prescription order but does allow a limited amount of anticipatory compounding based on previous dispensing history; this compounding must comply with USP <797>.
  • The FDA uses guidance documents to explain its position on a specific topic related to the design, production, labeling, promotion, manufacturing, and testing of regulated products.
  • The CMS is the agency that administers the country’s major healthcare programs, including Medicare and Medicaid.
  • The DEA summarizes the information applicable to pharmacies in the Pharmacist’s Manual: An Informational Outline of the Controlled Substances Act.
  • OSHA standards apply to all employers and include standards such as chemical exposure, electrical and fire safety, emergency action plan, exit routes, and medical and first aid.
  • It is illegal for an employer to fire, demote, transfer, or otherwise retaliate against an employee for filing a complaint with OSHA for observed health and safety violations in a workplace.
  • State boards of pharmacy (BOPs) are the primary regulators of pharmacy practice, and their purpose is to protect public health and safety.
  • Laws, regulations, and rules may vary among states but usually incorporate federal legislation.
  • State BOPs are responsible for licensing and regulating pharmacies that dispense medications to those states.
  • State BOPs are responsible for regulating pharmacists and pharmacy technicians within that state; determining requirements for licensure, registration, or certification; and establishing pharmacist-to-technician ratios.
  • Ethics are a set of moral responsibilities and qualities that pharmacy personnel must uphold in order to provide safe and effective treatment for the patients they serve.
  • Professional organizations often adopt a professional code of ethics that establishes a set of principles that guide pharmacy personnel in how they perform their work and in their relationships with patients, healthcare professionals, and society.
  • The Pharmacy Technician Certification Board (PTCB) requires certification candidates and certified pharmacy technicians (CPhTs) to agree to and uphold the PTCB Code of Conduct.
  • A quality assurance (QA) program is a formal system that includes policies, procedures, and other activities to ensure that established standards for maintaining the quality of CSPs are consistently met.
  • Quality control (QC) measures include performing an inspection of each CSP before it is released to determine whether its physical appearance is as expected.
  • Before initially preparing CSPs for patient administration, sterile compounding personnel must complete training, written testing, and competency assessments in sterile compounding.
  • Continuous Quality Improvement (CQI) involves using data from the QA programs and QC measurements as well as information from customer concerns or complaints to resolve problems and make system changes that will improve the overall confidence level that CSPs will be of appropriate identity, strength, purity, and quality.
  • Performing a gap analysis is a useful way to determine a facility’s level of compliance with USP <797>.
  • Facility-specific SOPs should direct every aspect of the sterile compounding process.
  • Facilities must identify a designated person(s) who are “assigned to be responsible and accountable for the performance and operation of the facility and personnel as related to the preparation of CSPs.”
